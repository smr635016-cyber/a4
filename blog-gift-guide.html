Gift Guide: Choosing a Watch as a Gift

A watch is a meaningful gift, but also one of the easier gifts to get subtly wrong — the right style for the wrong person, or a size that never quite fits comfortably. A little research ahead of time makes a real difference.

Pay attention to what they already wear

Someone who already wears a slim, minimal watch daily is unlikely to reach for a large, heavily featured chronograph, and the reverse is also true. Before choosing a style, look at what the person already gravitates toward in their existing accessories, rather than choosing based on what you personally find most appealing.

Case size matters more than most gift-givers realize

A watch that's noticeably too large or too small for someone's wrist will likely go unworn, however nice it looks in the box. If you're not confident about sizing, a moderate, versatile case size is a safer bet than an extreme size in either direction, or consider asking a mutual friend or family member discreetly rather than guessing.

Consider their lifestyle, not just their style

Someone who swims regularly or works with their hands needs different water resistance and durability than someone who wears a watch mainly at a desk. A beautiful, delicate dress watch is a poor gift for someone whose daily life would quickly damage it, regardless of how well it matches their taste.

Engraving adds meaning, but plan ahead

A short engraving on the case back — a date, initials, a brief message — can turn a nice watch into a genuinely sentimental keepsake. Engraving typically takes additional time to arrange, so factor this into your timeline if you're planning to have it done before a specific occasion.

When a gift card or store credit is the better choice

If you're genuinely unsure about someone's size or style preferences, a gift card or store credit toward a watch purchase is a reasonable and often appreciated alternative to guessing. This is especially true for a first quality watch purchase, where fit and personal preference matter enough that trying pieces on in person makes a real difference.

Buying for a milestone versus buying casually

A watch given for a specific milestone — a graduation, an anniversary, a retirement — often carries more weight if the choice reflects that occasion in some way, whether through an engraving, a specific style tied to the milestone, or simply the timing of the gift itself. A watch given without a specific occasion in mind can still be a wonderful gift, but the presentation and reasoning behind the choice matter less in that case.

Don't ignore the box and presentation

A watch's presentation box is a small but real part of the gifting experience, particularly for a significant purchase. If presentation matters for the occasion, check what packaging is included before assuming a simple pouch will feel appropriately special for a milestone gift.

The takeaway

A well-chosen watch gift comes down to matching the piece to the recipient's actual habits, wrist size, and lifestyle, not just picking something you personally find handsome. When in doubt about sizing or style, asking discreetly or opting for store credit is a better outcome than a beautiful watch that ends up sitting in a drawer.
